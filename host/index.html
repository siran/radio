Now playing — what is on the air, and the controls that change it for everyone listening
—
Your monitor — the music without you in it, so you can talk over it without hearing yourself a second late. Use headphones: on speakers this feeds straight back into the mic.
All three of these are this browser’s headphones. None of them touches what anybody else hears. The volume listeners hear you at is Mic volume on the mixing desk, which is a different control on a different panel and is on the air the moment it moves.
Use this when your voice lands EARLY — ahead of the beat you were aiming at. It holds this bed further behind the station, so you hear each beat later and therefore say your word later, which walks you back onto it. It moves the moment you let go, and only in your headphones — nothing about the air changes.
If your voice lands LATE, this is the wrong control and will make it worse. Late is fixed by Music held for the air on the mixing desk, which holds the music back for everybody and needs a restart. The arithmetic is one line:
how late you land = your monitor lag + your voice’s trip to the station − music held for the air
So set the desk knob PAST what you need, until you land early, and bring yourself back with this. Overshooting is the point: this slider can only add.
Hear my mic is your headphones only. Nothing in this panel reaches anybody else. To transmit, use Go on air at the top of the page.
The bed comes straight from liquidsoap and skips icecast, which hands every new listener four seconds of backlog. Your own mic is added here in the browser, at no delay at all — the station deliberately does not send your voice back, because hearing yourself a second late does not just distract, it breaks the sentence you are saying.

The queue — what is going to play, in order, once the track on the air finishes
The queue is what actually plays next. It lives inside liquidsoap and not in a file, so it does not survive a restart, and a track leaves it the moment it starts playing. A playlist, in the panel below, is a list you keep and plays nothing until you send it here.
    Find a track — search the library, then put what you find in the queue or in a playlist
    Searching by itself changes nothing and reaches nobody. A result goes where the Add to box says: the queue plays it, a playlist only keeps it.
      Playlists — lists you keep, saved on the server under a name
      A playlist is a list you keep, saved on the server in your own folder. It does not play. The queue, in its own panel above, is what is going to play, in that order, once what is on the air now finishes. Sending a playlist to the queue copies the tracks across, so changing the playlist afterwards does not change what will play.
